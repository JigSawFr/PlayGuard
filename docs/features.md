## Controls

↑/↓ move · Ⓐ confirm · Ⓑ back or cancel (twice on the sidebar to exit) · Ⓧ refresh (on the tabs that read the console) · Ⓨ notifications · **−** language and theme · **+** saves per-day limits (and, in Cadran, the week edited on *Play timer*).

What failed is said in a dialog, what worked in a toast. A dialog only says what applies to this console now: that the game in progress would be suspended, only while a game is open; that the companion app could overwrite a change, only while it is linked and can sync. An icon right of the title says while parental controls are temporarily unlocked (an open padlock) or when the app is read-only (a crossed-out pencil); a tap on it goes where it is undone (PIN › Turn parental controls back on, the firmware screen); in read-only mode, actions stay in place, greyed, and say why when pressed.

The app is organised in tabs, like System Settings; each has its section below.

## Two appearances

PlayGuard has two looks, chosen in *Preferences › Appearance* (taken at the next start):

- **Cadran**, the default: an icon rail for the tabs, a navy or cool-white palette (with the theme), cards instead of lists. Each tab opens on what matters most, and keeps every line of its classic version underneath:
  - *Today* (the Overview): the time left as a ring, and under it what stops play first (the time up if play goes on, or the bedtime alarm and when games are allowed again; while parental controls are unlocked, today's limit on hold and why); *Extra time* with its amounts one press away (an amount focused says when play would then end, or that the bedtime alarm still comes first), *Stop for today*, *Show the child*; the bedtime alarm, parental controls and the network clock as cards; the console's state as pills.
  - *Play timer*: the week is the editor. Ⓐ on a day edits it: ↑/↓ change it by 15 min, ←/→ move to the next day, Ⓛ copies it to Monday–Friday, Ⓡ to the weekend, Ⓨ sets no limit, Ⓐ or Ⓑ ends the edit; Ⓧ picks an exact time (*Enter a duration…* sets it with ↑/↓ by 15 min and Ⓛ/Ⓡ by 5 min, OK focused). By touch, a day tapped is edited, then a tap on its top or bottom changes it by 15 min. The changes are saved together, with **+** or *Save*, as in the per-day editor (the confirmation lists the days changed, before → after); *Discard* puts them back. Profiles, the same limit every day, bedtime, *When the time is up* and the removal are chips above and below it.
  - *Activity*: the accounts as chips, the period and the export, today, the last 7 days and all time as figures, the four games most played as cards (Ⓐ opens one). Nothing played over the period: a card says so, with *See all time* when there is something to see.
  - *Restrictions*: the four levels side by side, each with what it restricts (Ⓐ sets it; on *Custom* in force, Ⓐ goes to its settings below); no restriction under them.
  - **The child screen** (*Show the child* on *Today*): only the time left, the bedtime and what happens when time is up, to hand the console over. Ⓐ goes back to the parent's screens with the PIN; Ⓑ leaves PlayGuard (asking first to turn parental controls back on, as quitting does).
  - Dialogs are cards with their buttons as pills, the confirmation in teal (in coral when it removes or stops something); the choices open as a card above the hints, the current one in teal; messages appear as cards under the header, with a teal check for what was done and an amber mark for what was refused.
  - After a change is saved, **ZR** *Undo* stays in the footer for a few seconds: it opens that change in the change history, to put the previous value back. An empty change history says so in a card.
- **Classic**: the lists of earlier versions, as the screenshots above show them.

## Notifications — what needs you, from any tab

A bell in the header, left of the version, gathers what needs the parent; Ⓨ opens the list from any tab (the footer says *Notifications (2)* when two things are to do).

Left of the bell, the language (its code, *FR*, *EN*…) and the theme (a sun for light, a moon for dark, a half-filled circle when it follows the console): a tap opens the same list as *Preferences*, and **−** offers both from any tab. Either change takes effect once PlayGuard is started again.

- **To do**, counted on the bell in amber, stays until it is fixed: no PIN yet, a PIN anyone would try first (0000, 1234, 1212, 2580…: judged on the console, never shown), a PIN on the console while PlayGuard itself opens without it (*Protect PlayGuard with the PIN* on *Never*), parental controls temporarily unlocked (*Turn back on*), extra time from an earlier day still to put back, the "time's up" alarm off, changes made outside PlayGuard, the console not counting play time, an inaccurate network clock.
- **For information**, a dot on the bell, can be marked as read: a limit still lowered by *No more play today* on an earlier day (*Put back*), a new version installed (*What's new*; after an update in place, the previous build kept), a report sent online (*Show the link*), the week's play time (every 7 days until read) and a limit passed on two or more of the last 7 days while the console only warns, a settings backup to make (none yet, or none for 30 days), all games blocked for a week, a new user account, PlayGuard closed unexpectedly last time (*Send a report*), the companion app linked, a newer version of PlayGuard, the network clock found off at start-up.
- **Console**: the entries about the console rather than the parental controls come last, on one line that Ⓐ opens (open at once when nothing else is there). Those to do are counted on the Console tab's badge, not on the bell: installed games that cannot start (*Check the installed games*), game patches that need a look, the SD card almost full (under 500 MB), one of PlayGuard's own files that could not be put aside or written to the SD card, the recovery sysmodule out of date (not the one this version ships with, or installed before it said its version; *Update* puts in the one this version carries), then the console to restart for it, until it has, a build that did not finish starting and was gone back from (*Send a report*). Those for information: a build gone back from, a firmware not tested with this version, a newer Atmosphère, hekate or firmware (checked with *Check for updates every day*; the firmware's entry says to wait until Atmosphère supports it), the serial number not blanked on emuMMC, the other system's own parental controls, one of PlayGuard's own files found damaged and put aside (a toast at start-up points to it).
- Each section lists its entries in a fixed order, whichever check finished first.
- **In the sidebar**, an amber badge by a tab's name counts its entries to do, where they are fixed: the PIN tab (no PIN, a weak PIN, PlayGuard without its PIN, temporarily unlocked), the Play timer (the alarm off, the console not counting), the Overview (changes made outside PlayGuard, extra time to put back), the Console (its entries above, and the network clock). Ignored entries and those for information are not counted. In the tab, the same badge is on the row where each is fixed (*Network clock*, *Check the installed games*, *Storage*, *Game patches*; *Set or change the PIN*, *Protect PlayGuard with the PIN*, *Turn parental controls back on*; the Play timer's *Advanced* for the alarm), and the tab opens on the first of them.
- Ⓐ on an entry does the fix (*Turn it on*, *Set it*, *Update*…) or opens the tab where it is made. An entry goes by itself once what it says is fixed.
- Ⓧ marks an entry for information as read; Ⓨ marks them all. A few entries to do can be ignored with Ⓧ, after a confirmation and the PIN: a PIN anyone would try first, PlayGuard opening without the PIN, the "time's up" alarm off, the SD card almost full (never no PIN, unlocked controls, the clock, the play timer not counting or PlayGuard's own files). An ignored entry is no longer counted, and is listed last under *Ignored*, where Ⓐ counts it again; it comes back by itself 30 days later (unless *Count ignored warnings again after 30 days* is off), once what it says is fixed and then happens again, or, for the PIN, for a new PIN of another length.
- An entry for information marked as read stays read at the next start, until its text changes. *Preferences › Notifications* turns off three kinds: Atmosphère, hekate and firmware releases; the week's play time; reminders (backups, the other system, all games blocked).
- The state behind it is read when PlayGuard starts, every minute on the main screen, and every 5 s while the Overview is shown.

## Overview — today at a glance

- **Today first:** a gauge of today's play time (while no game is running, the time from the activity log, marked ≈), today's limit, time left, bedtime alarm. The "time's up" alarm shows in amber while it is off — Ⓐ turns it back on. Under the gauge, while a game is open (and parental controls are not unlocked), a reminder: a game left open on the HOME menu keeps counting (see [What counts as play time](#what-counts-as-play-time)).
- **Is the console counting?** PlayGuard's own time counts while it runs as an application, so with a limit today the time left should go down while it is open. When it has not moved for about 90 s on screen (timer on, a limit today, not unlocked, not opened from the album), an amber line says *The console is not counting play time right now*, with the likely causes: the clock was set back (it counts again later), the network clock was never set (*Console › Network clock*), or a limit was just written (start a game for a minute, then check). It goes as soon as the time left moves again. The Play timer's state line says it too.
- **Changed outside PlayGuard:** today's play time starting over on the same day (a clock change does that), the console clock moved by more than 5 minutes, limits that differ from the ones PlayGuard last saw (the phone app, another tool). An amber notice until dismissed or the next day, and an entry in the change history. PlayGuard does not run in the background: it only sees what changed while it was open, or since it last was, when it reads the console again.
- **At the limit** the console shows "Time's up", then suspends the game (no "continue") or only warns: *When the time is up* shows which and changes it, once a limit or a bedtime is set ([details](parental-controls.md#when-the-time-runs-out)).
- **Then the state:** parental controls, PIN (*Set* or *Not set*, never its length), restriction level.
- **Then what needs attention:** network-clock accuracy, companion-app link (amber while linked), firmware / compatibility only when there is a problem, serial blanking and game patches warnings.
- Lines that open another tab end with a chevron (›). Ⓐ on *Today's limit* changes it in place; Ⓐ on *Network clock › Inaccurate* measures and sets the clock on the spot.
- **Extra time today** (+15 min, +30 min, +1 h by default) and **No more play today** (limit 0 for today only, *now* or *in 5 minutes* when the time played is known: the limit then becomes the time played + 5 min, time to save; at the lock the console's "Time's up" comes up, and unsaved progress may be lost — the confirmation says so). The next day — at start-up, or at midnight if the app is open — PlayGuard puts the usual limit back. After extra time it does so by itself, with no question and no PIN (it only lowers the limit, as turning parental controls back on does), unlocking for a moment if the timer is counting and locking again at once; it waits while it cannot write (read-only mode, a firmware it does not support, the console lock on) and, a week later on the same day, while the lower limit would stop the game in progress (then the next day). To keep more time on a day, change that day's limit. After *No more play today*, it asks whether to keep 0 or put the limit back (through the PIN, as any change that raises a limit), or puts it back as any change (*Play timer › Put the limit back by itself after "No more play today"*); "no limit" to put back is always asked.
- A **Turn back on** banner while parental controls are temporarily unlocked.
- While no PIN is set, a **First steps** line opens the guide (three steps, plus one each while the phone app is linked, while the "time's up" alarm is off and once a PIN is set: up to six), which also comes up at start-up.
- Refreshes every 5 s, with the time of the last refresh (Ⓧ refreshes now).

## Play timer — the week chart is the editor

- A state line: active, time left, today's limit, the matching profile (red while the limit is reached).
- **The week chart:** ←/→ pick a day, Ⓐ opens the per-day editor on it, its picker already open. The editor is the one place for a limit per day: change several days, they stay in amber and marked \* until **+** (from anywhere) saves them all at once, with one confirmation; quick values, "no limit" per day, Monday–Friday / weekend presets, copy today to every day.
- **Same limit every day:** a quick list or any value, typed in minutes (`90`, `90 min`) or hours (`1:30`, `1h30`).
- **Weekdays and weekend ›:** the editor, asking for Monday–Friday then for the weekend.
- **Remove the limit**, **extra time today**, **no more play today** (as on the Overview).
- A note under the daily limit says what the console does at the limit (as on the Overview). While parental controls are temporarily unlocked, play time *may* not be counted: that is still open ([docs](parental-controls.md#still-open)).
- **Profiles** saved on the SD card: apply (a profile already in force just says so), edit, rename or delete one; save the current limits or make a new one. Any name, in any script (*École*, *周末*, *Выходные* …) — a name without Latin letters gets a file name of its own, and two names that would map to the same file are caught.
- Every confirmation draws the week as it will be, with the days that change in amber.
- **Bedtime alarm**: the alarm time (16:00 to 23:45, or off) and when play is allowed again (05:00 to 09:00), the same every day. Its place in the play-timer settings, worked out from the companion app's settings, is verified on a console (22.0.0); PlayGuard still changes it only once the console reports what PlayGuard reads there, checks the console's answer after the change and puts the previous settings back if it differs.
- **Extra time:** the amounts offered (+15/+30/+1 h, +10/+20/+30 min …) and putting the usual limit back without asking after *No more play today* (extra time always comes back without asking). Either is done the next time PlayGuard is opened, from the next day on: a console it is not opened on keeps the limit of that day.
- **Advanced ›**, a screen of its own: the switch for the debug-class actions and, while it is on, the "time's up" alarm on/off and pause / resume the countdown. The same switch lets a backup or the history put the alarm back.
- `0` minutes means *no play that day*; *Remove the play-time limit* turns the timer off.

## Activity — who played what, and how long

- Time per game **today**, in the **last 7 days** and **in all**, from the console's own activity log — for every account or **one user account** (*Account*, when the console has several). PlayGuard starts on the account shown last, or on the one set under *Account by default* (every account, or one).
- A chart of the last seven days (today on the right, a legend under it), with this week's limit for each day as a line and the time over it in amber, marked !; today's, the week's and all-time totals.
- A **summary** over the chosen period, for the account shown: average per day, the most played game, the days played and the busiest one (last 7 days), the average session (all time).
- **All games**, on a screen of their own: every game played over the period, most played first, as a **grid of covers** or a **list** (Ⓨ switches, and the choice is kept), with the selected game's figures always on top: its icon and name, the period's play time, all time, launches and last play. Ⓧ picks the period (the Activity tab follows). Icons are read around the selected game (not in applet mode, to spare memory); a grey controller stands in until one arrives, and for a game without one. A large library shows its first 50 games, then *Show every game* (100 games at most in applet mode); the export always holds them all. PlayGuard reads 256 games at most (this week's first): beyond that, the Activity tab, All games and the export (not CSV) say that some are missing, and the all-time total leaves them out.
- Ⓐ on a game: its last seven days as bars, launches, first and last play, time per user account. Deleted games keep their all-time figures, even one deleted before PlayGuard was installed as long as the console's play log still names it, and their name once PlayGuard has seen it (else *Deleted game* and its title ID).
- **Time on no account and new accounts:** a game's time that no user account holds (some games never ask for one, or the account was deleted since) shows as *No account*, from a minute up; a user account PlayGuard never saw before is flagged in amber under *Account* for 30 days (*New account since …*, a fresh account is a known way to start over) and recorded in the change history; the accounts there when PlayGuard first lists them are not flagged.
- **Export to the SD card** as CSV, JSON, XLSX (Excel) or PDF, one column per day.
- Opens at once on the last figures read (kept on the SD card between runs), refreshed in the background when over a minute old; Ⓧ reads them again now, with a spinner.
- Times are approximate if the console clock was changed.

## Restrictions — level, age rating, communication

- Restriction level: None, Young child, Child, Teen, Custom. What a preset restricts (age limit, posting, communication) is shown read-only, and said before you pick it.
- In Custom: age rating, social-media posting, communication with others.
- VR mode, **rating organisation** (PEGI, ESRB, USK, CERO …).
- The number of games allowed to communicate (the list itself is in the diagnostic report, raw, until its layout is known).

## PIN — the PIN, unlocking, blocking games, companion app

- **Set / change the PIN** (system PIN screen), **show the PIN** (after a warning and the PIN itself, asked every time; recorded in the history, then a new PIN is offered), **unlock temporarily**, **turn parental controls back on**, and whether PlayGuard turns them back on by itself right after a change that needed the unlock (on by default).
- **Protect PlayGuard with the PIN** in PlayGuard itself: *Never*, *Before a change* (the default: anyone can look, only the parent changes something) or *To open PlayGuard* (a lock screen at start). Once entered, the PIN holds until PlayGuard closes; *Ask for the PIN again after 5 minutes* (off by default) asks again after them, and brings *To open PlayGuard*'s lock screen back when PlayGuard returns after 5 min or more out of focus. Checked in the service layer, so no change skips it; locking again never asks. *Show the PIN* asks for it every time, in every mode and even right after it was entered, and a missing or damaged `config.json` counts as *Before a change*. Quitting while parental controls are still unlocked asks whether to lock them again (*Turn back on and quit* also closes the game PlayGuard was opened over, back to the HOME menu rather than hbmenu); closed from the HOME menu instead, PlayGuard locks them again the next time it starts.
- **Block all games:** one switch that sets every day's limit to 0, so a PIN is needed to start a game — a light block without age ratings or communication limits. It blocks starting games, not the HOME menu, and needs a PIN. The previous limits come back when it is turned off. While it is on, *extra time* and *no more play today* are refused, and limits set another way (a profile, a backup, the history…) replace it.
- **Companion app:** whether the Nintendo Switch Parental Controls app is linked, its last sync, and **unlink** (otherwise its next sync overwrites the limits set here: while it is linked, every change PlayGuard asks to confirm says so). When Atmosphère's DNS redirection (`atmosphere/hosts`) sends Nintendo's parental-control server elsewhere, the app cannot synchronize: PlayGuard then says *sync blocked (DNS)* here and mentions the app nowhere else (no note in the dialogs, no bell entry, no first-steps step, no pill on *Today*, no help to decode, and unlinking asks without saying what the app would overwrite). While it is linked and can sync, *Before unlinking: help decode…* opens the block comparison (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#no-code-needed)).
- **Delete all parental controls:** two confirmations, irreversible; a backup of the settings is saved first.

## Console — the network clock, the installed games, what the console runs

- **Network clock ›**, a screen of its own — the clock the play timer relies on:
  - Console and network clocks, time zone, accuracy. When Atmosphère's DNS redirection (`atmosphere/hosts`) sends Nintendo's time server (`aauth-lp1.ndas.srv.nintendo.net`) elsewhere, the console can no longer set its network clock itself: this screen, the bell's entry and *Today*'s card say so and point to the measurement below.
  - Pick a public NTP server (≈ 50 built in, by region, or your own).
  - **Measure** against 3 servers (median; an amber warning when they disagree), then **set the network clock** — a measurement stays usable for 2 minutes, with a countdown. The confirmation says it: changing the clock starts today's play time over, so the whole limit is available again.
  - A console that never reaches Nintendo's servers keeps this clock inaccurate, which skews the play timer.
  - **No server answers** (a console without Internet): *Set the network clock to the console clock* appears under the result (and on the Overview's *Inaccurate* line, after its measurement fails), behind the same confirmation. PlayGuard has nothing else to compare the console clock with: the confirmation shows its time and asks you to check it against a phone or a watch first, and a console clock before 2026 (never set) is refused. With *Synchronize Clock via Internet* off, the console clock stays where it is, and turning the setting on afterwards no longer makes it jump; with it on, the console clock already follows the network clock, so the confirmation says to turn it off and set the time by hand if it is wrong. It writes the clock only, not the parental controls, so it needs no unlock (the PIN of *Protect PlayGuard with the PIN* still applies); like any clock change, it starts today's play time over. Not tried on a console yet.
  - A network-clock check at start-up (a toast when it is more than a minute off; it never sets the clock).
- **Installed games:** the games the HOME menu cannot start or draw — an icon that keeps loading, a "!", a dotted frame — with what is wrong (name and icon unreadable, the game itself missing behind its update, nothing installed any more, files missing, corrupt data the console found, a newer firmware needed by the game or only by its update, keys too new for the firmware, a missing license (ticket), the console asking for an update that was removed, the SD card unreadable, a launch the console refuses) and what to do, under the game's icon, with how many installed games have a problem. The list has a section per way out: **Fix here** (what PlayGuard repairs), **To check** (data the console suspects: the check says whether to install the game again), **Reinstall** (files gone or unusable, or data found corrupt: remove, then install again) and **Firmware or SD card** (the game is fine, the console is not), each with its count and what to do, the games with the same problem together; two games with the same name are told apart by their version or title ID. What the data check found is kept with the game until another version is installed: corrupt, it moves to *Reinstall* as *Corrupt (checked)*, with the date and the console's code; intact, it leaves the list. *Check these games* checks the whole of *To check* one game after the other in one dialog (*game 2 of 5*), then says how many are corrupt, intact or could not be checked, with removing the corrupt ones at hand. Ⓨ picks games (a check mark in front), and *Several games at once*, above the sections, picks a whole section; then one action for all of them: check their data, or remove them (their list saved first, one question, one PIN). Under *Reinstall*, their list can be saved (JSON and text, in the exports folder: name, title ID, version, problem), and all of them removed at once after one question and one PIN: the list is saved first, then the games are removed so an installer no longer counts them as installed. When one action is the fix, it is the dialog's first button; the **other actions** follow: accept the installed version again (when the console asks for a removed update), remove only the update or an add-on, check the data for corruption (the console's own check, with its progress and Cancel), or remove the game. Versions are shown as the game shows them (*version 1.4.0*, *update #4*); after a repair, a toast says whether the game works again. Save data always stays; every change is in the change history. The last check shows at once; then only the games whose entry changed are checked again (every game once a day, after a firmware update, or with Ⓧ *Check again*), each shown as soon as it is checked. Several broken games at once points to the SD card or to a game installed from the other storage (sysMMC / emuMMC). *View*, above the sections, shows the games as a **list** or as **covers** (icon, name and problem; the choice is kept), as *All games* does.
- **Console:** firmware, Atmosphère and hekate (when its `bootloader/update.bin` is on the SD card), each with the newer release the last update check found, in amber (*22.0.0 · 23.0.1 available*, and how many versions behind when more than one), and a warning icon on the firmware's row when the newest firmware needs a newer Atmosphère or hekate first, or is not supported by them yet (the firmware's release notes say why: their release notes say which firmware they support). Ⓐ on each opens its **release notes**: a tab for the installed version, one for each newer one (at most five) and older ones in the tabs left (six in all; Ⓛ / Ⓡ move between them), opening on the next one, with tags (installed or newer, the newest firmware it supports, security fixes, parental controls changed), the notes as published (in English), *See online* (a QR code) and when they were fetched. Atmosphère's and hekate's come from their GitHub releases, the firmware's from switchbrew; they are kept on the SD card for offline reading, fetched again once a day (Ⓧ now). Then compatibility, storage (emuMMC or sysMMC, with an info icon when the other system has its own parental controls: Ⓐ explains), whether Atmosphère **blanks the serial number** (partly hidden until Ⓐ; on emuMMC, when it is not, a warning icon on the row above, which Ⓐ explains), **game patches** (sys-patch or sigpatch files, recommending sys-patch when only files are used: an info icon, or a warning one when something needs a look, which Ⓐ explains).

## History — changes and backups

- **Change history:** the newest 500 changes PlayGuard made (limits, restriction level, PIN, unlocks, unlinking, the clock, restores, pausing or resuming the countdown …) and what it saw changed outside it, grouped by day (*Today*, *Yesterday*, then the day and date), with the time and from where; a limit changed on one day names that day. A change the console **refused** is listed too, in amber, with the console's error and what was tried (never put back: nothing changed). *Show* keeps one kind (play time, restrictions, security and app, seen outside PlayGuard, refused by the console); *Export what is shown* writes it to `exports/` as CSV, JSON, XLSX or PDF. Each change keeps the PlayGuard version that made it. Ⓐ on a change shows it and, for a value, **puts the previous one back** — through the same unlock and PIN as any change, saying if it changed since. A change recorded by a newer version of PlayGuard shows as *Other change*.
- **Back up / restore the settings** on the SD card: restriction level, custom settings, VR mode, rating organisation, daily limits, the "time's up" alarm (with the advanced actions on), and the raw play-timer block for the record — never the PIN. A restore lists only what would change (nothing to change: it just says so, with no unlock and nothing written), and says when the backup had a bedtime alarm on (it is not written back). Choose how many backups to keep.

## Preferences

- What sets PlayGuard itself up:
  - Language (every console language, or the console's own), theme (light / dark) and appearance (Cadran / Classic, see above), each with an offer to restart.
  - The tab to start on.
  - **The intro** at start-up: the loading screen for 2 seconds (on by default; Ⓐ or Ⓑ skips it; off, PlayGuard opens as soon as it is ready).
- The messages it shows:
  - The kinds of notifications (see above).
  - A **monthly reminder to support PlayGuard** (on by default, never in the first month nor right after an update; *Don't show again* on the reminder or this switch turns it off for good, updates included).
- A setting that shapes one feature sits with it: extra time in *Play timer*, turning parental controls back on in *PIN*, the clock check in *Console › Network clock*. The updates and *Help & diagnostics* are in *About*.

## About

- The installed PlayGuard: version (in amber when a newer one is out; Ⓐ opens its **release notes**), launch mode and data folder; **check for updates** now; *Check for updates every day* at start-up, and *Update with*: in PlayGuard, which downloads the release, checks it, replaces itself and restarts, or sphaira, Homebrew App Store or by hand (asked on the first update when none is chosen). While a newer release is known, PlayGuard opens on **PlayGuard X.Y.Z is available** (its date and notes): *Update*, or *Later* for 1 day, 1 week or 30 days at most; the release notes are PlayGuard's own, on the same screen as Atmosphère's and hekate's in *Console*: from its GitHub releases, a tab per version (the installed one, the newer ones, the older ones), each with its highlights as cards and every change, kept on the SD card for offline reading (the installed version's are in the app too, for when nothing was fetched yet). After an update, PlayGuard opens once on **What's new in X.Y.Z** (the same highlights, then the QR codes).
- **Help & diagnostics ›**, a screen of its own, below *Update with*:

## Safety by design

**Writing the play-time limit safely.** Overwriting the timer's configuration while it counts down destabilises Atmosphère. So PlayGuard checks the state first; if the timer is active, the confirmation says parental controls will be unlocked temporarily — with the stored PIN, **you don't need to remember it**. One press then unlocks, checks that the system really reports the unlock, writes, and **locks again right away** (or offers to, if *Turn parental controls back on after a change* is off). The service layer re-checks the state just before writing, so no screen can skip the safeguard.

> [!CAUTION]
> A limit below the time already played today suspends the game as soon as parental controls are locked again. The confirmation says so when that would happen.

**No more 22.5 crashes.** `pctl:a`, the privileged parental-control service, accepts a **single session**. Older builds of the original app kept it open, so the HOME-menu PIN prompt (or the PIN applet) could not get it and Atmosphère could crash. PlayGuard opens the session for each action and releases it immediately; periodic refreshes pause while the app is in the background. *(Diagnosis by [anbingxi's fork](https://github.com/anbingxi/NX-Pctl-Manager/tree/diag/fw22-5-readonly).)*

**Nothing runs in the background.** The limit, the PIN, the warnings and the suspension are the console's own; PlayGuard only changes their settings.

## What counts as play time

The play timer is the console's: PlayGuard reads it, it does not count anything itself. What is known, and how sure it is ([parental-controls.md](parental-controls.md#time-spent-and-time-left) has the measurements):

- A game running — Yes *(measured (22.0.0))*
- **A game left open on the HOME menu** (suspended in the background) — **Yes**: close it, or put the console in sleep mode *(reported by parents ([Arqade](https://gaming.stackexchange.com/questions/398954/)); fits the measurements (the console counts while an application is open))*
- Sleep mode — No *(reported ([Arqade](https://gaming.stackexchange.com/questions/398954/)); not measured by PlayGuard)*
- **PlayGuard opened over a game** (as an application) — **Yes**, as that game *(measured (22.0.0): open it with a parent's user, or from the album for a quick look)*
- PlayGuard opened from the album (applet mode) — No *(inferred: it is not an application, and time outside applications did not seem to count)*
- While parental controls are temporarily unlocked — Maybe not *(not measured: still open)*
- After the clock was changed — The day starts over; after a jump back the count stops until the clock passes the old time *(observed (22.0.0); the Overview says when the console is not counting)*
